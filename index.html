About

A software engineer who builds the systems a business runs on — and ships them.

I'm the in-house software engineer at Jeru Ventures, an FMCG importer in Nairobi. I build their business software end to end: an ERP that takes a sale from a rep's field visit to dispatch and a VAT-compliant invoice, a GPS-verified field-team app, a browser-based call centre, and a team of AI agents for marketing work.

I also founded Convexity Labs — a live, real-time options analytics terminal that streams market data over WebSockets, models dealer gamma exposure and archives order flow tick by tick. I designed, built and run it solo, on Oracle Cloud with CI/CD.

Before that I freelanced across MERN and Python data work, and years in sales and IT support taught me to build for the people who actually use the system.

Questions

What people usually ask before they get in touch.

Who is Peter Njuguna?

Peter Njuguna is a software engineer based in Nairobi, Kenya. He is the in-house engineer at Jeru Ventures, an FMCG importer, where he builds an ERP, AI agents and a browser call centre, and he is the founder of Convexity Labs, a live real-time options analytics terminal.

What does Peter Njuguna specialise in?

Full-stack and backend engineering for systems that run a business: Python and FastAPI, TypeScript with React and Next.js, PostgreSQL and Supabase, real-time data over WebSockets, and LLM agents with retrieval-augmented generation. He ships to production with Docker and CI/CD on Oracle Cloud and Vercel.

Is he a good fit for a backend or FastAPI role in Nairobi?

Yes. Convexity Labs runs a FastAPI and Redis backend that streams live Deribit and Binance market data over WebSockets and archives every trade to TimescaleDB. He vectorised its analytics in NumPy, cutting one calculation from 2,895 ms to 288 ms and another from 5.34 ms to 0.41 ms.

Has he built AI agents or LLM applications?

Yes. At Jeru Ventures he built a team of LLM agents that research, plan and review marketing work. They are grounded in company knowledge through hybrid retrieval with pgvector, pass a claims gate before anything is published, run under a budget guard, and sit on a provider-agnostic model layer.

What business systems has he shipped?

An ERP that takes a sale from a rep's field visit to dispatch, a VAT-compliant invoice and credit notes, with row-level security and live dashboards; a GPS-verified field-team app with offline support; and a browser call centre on Asterisk and WebRTC with IVR, queues and recordings.
